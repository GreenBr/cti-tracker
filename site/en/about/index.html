About

What this site is

An automatically updated tracker of cyber attack activity related to the People's Republic of China, in both directions: campaigns by China-attributed threat actors, and attacks against targets inside China. It is a personal research tool; nothing here is original attribution research.

How it works

  1. Fetch — the pipeline pulls the latest articles from public security sources (vendor research blogs, government advisories, security news) via RSS, in English and Chinese.
  2. Filter — articles are pre-screened by keywords: the names and aliases of China-attributed groups imported from MITRE ATT&CK, plus geographic terms. Matching articles become candidates.
  3. Extract — each candidate article is read by an LLM, which records every attack it describes in a fixed schema: actor, victim country and sector, date, direction, MITRE ATT&CK techniques, and a confidence level. Malformed answers are rejected, and irrelevant articles yield no incidents.
  4. Publish — the database is rendered into this static site in three languages. Every incident links back to the article it came from.

Update schedule

The pipeline runs automatically once a day, at a fixed time: 09:00 (UTC+8). If the computer hosting it is off at that moment, that day's run is skipped and the site keeps the previous data.

Reading the data

This site aggregates public reporting. Every claim carries the confidence its source stated, and should be checked against the linked original before being relied on. Coverage is only as good as the source list: Chinese-language sources are currently under-represented, so the "against China" direction is sparse.

Content policy

Only titles, metadata and our own short summaries are published here. Full article text is never republished; follow the source links for the originals.
